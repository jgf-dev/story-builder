To elevate the Gemini TTS output of "I Came During Tryouts" into a high-end, immersive audio drama (often referred to as an "enhanced audiobook" or "binaural audio play"), you will need a targeted collection of sound assets in your DAW (such as Reaper, Logic Pro, Pro Tools, or Audacity).

Here is a comprehensive breakdown of the post-production DAW sound assets, categorized by scene, along with the exact types of ambient beds, foley, vocal chains, and convolution reverb impulses you should source or record.

1. Acoustic Ambiances (Ambient Beds)
These are continuous, looping background tracks (usually 2 to 5 minutes long) mixed at a very low volume (around -24dB to -35dB) to glue the voices and foley together.

Gymnasium Room Tone (Scenes 1 & 2):
Asset: An empty or semi-active high school gymnasium bed.
Key Textures: Faint, low-frequency HVAC hum, distant fluorescent light buzz, and the unique "hollow" silence of a massive room.
Active Shower Room / Wet Locker Room Bed (Scene 3):
Asset: Continuous running water hitting tiled floors.
Key Textures: Multiple shower heads running at varying distances, occasional hollow metallic clinks (pipes heating up), and the soft, continuous splashing of water.
Suburban Outdoor Afternoon Bed (Scene 4):
Asset: Faint open-air neighborhood ambiance.
Key Textures: A very light wind rustling through trees, distant suburban traffic (to establish a sense of being outside the school gate), and a faint bird chirp or two.
2. Foley & Spot Effects (The Physical Actions)
Foley brings the physical contact of wrestling and the locker room to life. These should be precisely aligned with the TTS dialogue and stage directions.

Athletic Gear & Mat Sounds (Scenes 1 & 2):
Squeaking Athletic Shoes: Quick, high-pitched rubber squeaks on gym floors or wrestling mats to represent circling and footwork.
Heavy Mat Thuds: Low-frequency, heavy, muffled "whumps"—representing Cody being thrown or pinned down. (Tip: You can record this by dropping a heavy laundry bag or punch bag onto a dense mattress).
Fabric Friction / Rustle: Nylon shorts and polyester athletic shirts rubbing aggressively against each other. This is crucial for Scene 2 when Sam is grinding his hips/knees into Cody's back.
Wet Locker Room Foley (Scene 3):
Bare Feet on Wet Tile: Sticky, squeaky skin-on-tile footsteps.
Wet Skin Slapping / Shoving: Soft, fleshy "slap" and "slide" sounds to represent the fake chokehold, body-on-body contact, and gentle struggling.
Soap Squeals: The sound of hands rubbing together with thick, wet lather.
Metal Locker Doors: Distant, heavily reverberated metal door slams to remind the listener they are in a locker room.
Outdoor Walk Foley (Scene 4):
Gravel Walking: Rhythmic footsteps crunching over gravel and asphalt at a leisurely, synchronized walking pace.
Bicycle Click / Roll: Ryan is waiting by the bike rack; a faint bicycle chain click or the sound of a rubber tire rolling slowly on pavement as they walk adds great environmental context.
Friendly Shove: A soft fabric rustle and slight exhale when Ryan shoves Cody.
3. Convolution Reverb Impulse Responses (IRs)
Convolution reverb uses real-world acoustic prints (Impulse Responses) to make the TTS voices sound like they were recorded in the actual spaces.

Scene Reverb IR Type Mix Setting (Wet/Dry) Pre-Delay Production Notes
Scene 1 & 2 (Gym) Large High School Gym / Basketball Court 12% - 15% Wet 35ms - 45ms High pre-delay keeps the dialogue clear while letting the "tail" bloom in the background.
Scene 3 (Showers) Tiled Public Restroom / Indoor Swimming Pool 25% - 35% Wet 10ms - 15ms High wetness. Highly reflective walls cause a shorter pre-delay and a bright, metallic "slap-back" echo.
Scene 4 (Outside) Dry (No Reverb) or "Outdoor Street" 2% - 5% Wet 0ms Keep it dry. The brain registers "outdoors" by the absence of room reflections.
4. Vocal DSP Processing Chains (The Secret Sauce)
Because the Gemini voices (Enceladus, Orus, Sadachbia) are synthesized, applying a warm, analog-style vocal chain will make them sound incredibly human and expensive.

The "Cody" (Enceladus) Chain (Vulnerable / Breathless)
Cody's performance relies on whispers, gasps, and heavy breathing.

De-Esser: Set aggressively around 5kHz–7kHz. Breathy voices can produce harsh "S" and "T" sounds.
Tube Saturation (e.g., Soundtoys Decapitator or FabFilter Saturn): Add a tiny touch of tube warmth. This adds harmonic weight to his soft, nervous tone.
Dynamic EQ / Multiband Compression: Tame the 250Hz–400Hz range dynamically. When Cody whispers closely to the mic (Scene 2), the "proximity effect" can make his voice sound muddy; dynamic EQ keeps it clear.
The "Sam" (Orus) Chain (Deep / Dominant)
Sam is deep, confident, and whispers directly into Cody's ear.

Sub-Harmonic Synthesizer (e.g., Waves LoAir): Apply a tiny amount of sub-bass enhancement below 80Hz only during his close whispered lines in Scene 2. This creates an intimate, heavy, "in-your-head" physical presence.
Optical Compressor (e.g., CLA-2A or Teletronix LA-2A): Smooth out his delivery. A slow attack and release will make his voice sound velvety and effortless.
The "Spatialization" Hack (Binaural Audio)
For Scene 2 (the pin) and Scene 3 (the shower chokehold), use a 3D/Binaural Panner (such as DearVR Micro, Sennheiser AMBEO Orbit, or Logic Pro’s Binaural Panner):

Keep Cody’s internal monologue centered (0° azimuth) and completely dry to sound like it is happening "inside the listener's head."
Pan Sam’s whispered lines slightly to the Left Rear (-110°) or Right Rear (+110°) and keep them extremely dry and close. This mimics the feeling of someone breathing directly into your left/right ear while pinning you down from behind, creating an intense, visceral listening experience.
